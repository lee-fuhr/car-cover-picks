How to choose a car covers

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

To choose a waterproof car cover, start by matching the cover’s material and construction to your primary climate and storage situation, outdoor storage in rainy or snowy areas demands a multi-layer fabric with a breathable waterproof membrane, while indoor use can often get by with a simple dust cover. Prioritize a custom-fit model with secure tie-downs and a soft inner lining to protect the paint, and always verify that the cover is both waterproof and breathable to avoid trapped moisture, which can damage the finish. Read verified owner reviews for real-world feedback on fit, durability, and how well the cover sheds water over time.

What type of car cover material is truly waterproof?

True waterproofing in a car cover comes from either a laminated polyurethane layer or a heavy-duty polyester fabric with a durable water-repellent coating. The most reliable outdoor covers use a three-layer construction: a non-abrasive inner fleece or felt, a breathable water-resistant middle layer, and a tough waterproof outer shell. Be cautious of covers that claim to be waterproof but are simply a thin nylon sheet, without breathability, those can turn your car into a greenhouse of trapped condensation. Many respected brands offer covers made from woven polypropylene that is both waterproof and breathable, often with added UV inhibitors. Owner feedback consistently shows that a cover with sealed seams and reinforced corners performs much better in heavy rain. Stay away from cheap single-ply plastic covers; they degrade in sunlight and can leave sticky residue on your paint.

Indoor vs. outdoor: does it matter for a waterproof cover?

Yes, it matters a great deal. If your car lives outside full-time, you need a cover that can handle rain, snow, bird droppings, and tree sap, that means a thick, multi-layer fabric with a waterproof outer shell and a soft inner lining to prevent scratches. A cover designed for outdoor use typically has reinforced grommets for tie-down straps and a vented panel to release hot air on sunny days. For indoor storage, a waterproof cover is often overkill unless you worry about leaks in your garage or condensation from changes in humidity. A simple lightweight dust cover that’s breathable is usually fine. However, if you store your car in a carport with open sides, treat it as outdoor storage and choose a waterproof cover. The wrong choice can lead to mildew or moisture damage, so match the cover to your storage reality.

How important is a custom fit for a waterproof cover?

A custom-fit cover is one of the most important features for long-term protection. Universal covers may save money, but they often flap in the wind, letting rain sneak under the edges and causing wear marks on the finish. Custom covers are tailored to your car’s exact shape, including mirrors, antennas, and rear spoilers, so they stay in place and shed water effectively. Owner reviews frequently note that a snug fit also helps the cover stay put during high winds, especially when paired with proper tie-down straps. Many brand-name covers offer semi-custom fits that accommodate a range of similar models, but for the best waterproof performance a model-specific cover is worth the investment. Check the manufacturer’s fit guide and read reviews from owners of the same vehicle before buying.

What about breathability and condensation?

Breathability is the unsung hero of a good waterproof cover. Without it, moisture trapped inside the cover can condense on your paint, leading to mildew, peeling clear coat, or even rust. The best cover fabrics combine a waterproof outer layer with a breathable inner membrane that allows air to circulate while keeping rain out. This is often called a “waterproof yet breathable” construction, similar to a high-end rain jacket. Look for covers that include vents or grommets near the front and rear of the cover to promote airflow. Many top-rated covers from brands like Covercraft or WeatherTech use a fabric that is both waterproof and highly breathable. Avoid any cover that feels like a rubber tarp, it will seal too tightly and cause problems. In very humid climates, some owners report still getting light condensation even with a breathable cover, but it’s far less harmful than trapped water.

How to secure a cover against wind?

Wind is a car cover’s worst enemy. A flapping cover can sandpaper your paint, snap loose, or even blow away. Look for covers with integrated elastic hems, reinforced grommets, and a set of strong tie-down straps that run under the car. Some covers include a cable lock that goes through the grommets and secures around the car’s undercarriage, which also acts as a theft deterrent. Reportedly, the best outdoor covers have a four- or six-strap system with quick-release buckles. Owners of vehicles parked in exposed areas often add bungee cords and extra wind straps for peace of mind. For extra security against high winds, a cover with a built-in antenna patch or mirror pockets that fit tightly reduces flapping. If you live in a particularly windy area, consider a cover that is slightly heavier and has a fiber content that resists tearing when the wind pulls on it.

What features protect paint from scratches?

The inner layer of the cover is just as important as the waterproof outer. Choose a cover with a soft fleece, flannel, or microfiber lining that will not scratch your clear coat. Some covers also include a “ten-layer” or “high-density” construction where each layer serves a purpose, one for dirt resistance, another for scratch prevention, and so on. Be wary of covers with a rough canvas inner, as they can leave micro-abrasions over time. Also consider that dirt and dust can get trapped under the cover. Even a soft lining can cause swirl marks if you put the cover on a dirty car. Many experienced owners recommend rinsing the car before covering it, especially with a waterproof cover that will trap debris. Look for covers that are machine-washable or easy to briefly rinse off, and always check that the cover’s seams are smooth and don’t have rough stitching on the inside that could contact the paint.

Frequently asked questions

Can a waterproof car cover cause rust?

Only if it traps moisture against the body. A good waterproof cover is also breathable, letting condensation escape. Non-breathable covers can indeed create a greenhouse effect that promotes rust, so always choose one that explicitly says “breathable waterproof” rather than just “waterproof.”

How often should I remove the cover?

If you use the car daily, remove the cover each time you drive and reinstall it after parking. For long-term storage, try to remove the cover every few weeks on a dry day to let the car air out and check for any moisture or debris underneath. Frequent removal also lets you inspect the paint for any signs of trapped grit.

Are cheap covers worth it?

Generally no. A budget cover under a few hundred dollars often uses thin non-breathable material that can damage paint or fail within a year. It may save money upfront, but you’ll likely replace it quickly or risk costly paint repairs. Investing in a mid-range cover from a reputable brand gives better material, fit, and long-term value.

Do covers work for snow and ice?

Yes, the best outdoor waterproof covers handle snow and ice well. Look for a cover with a thick material that won’t tear under ice weight, plus reinforced seams. Some covers are advertised as “snow-proof” or “ice-resistant,” meaning snow slides off more easily. You may need to brush off heavy snow occasionally to prevent strain on the cover’s fabric.

Do I need a cover if I have a garage?

If your garage is dry and dust-free, you probably only need a lightweight dust cover rather than a fully waterproof one. But if the garage is damp or leaks, a waterproof cover with good breathability can prevent moisture damage and protect against accidental scratches from garage clutter. Many owners skip the cover entirely in a clean, climate-controlled garage.

How to clean a car cover?

Most covers can be hand-washed or machine-washed on a gentle cycle with mild soap. Avoid bleach or fabric softeners, as they break down waterproof coatings. Air-dry the cover completely before storing it, never put it away wet, as that leads to mildew. Always follow the manufacturer’s care instructions, as some high-end covers require special handling.

In shortChoosing the right waterproof car cover comes down to honestly assessing your climate, storage situation, and how much protection your car’s finish truly needs. Start with a custom-fit, breathable waterproof cover from a brand with a strong reputation among owners, and pair it with proper tie-downs and regular maintenance. Spend a little more now, and your car’s paint and interior will thank you for years to come.
